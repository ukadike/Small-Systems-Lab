# 04 — Research Questions

## Primary research question

How can an accessibility-first creative technology studio prepare students to design, build, and evaluate public-interest systems that strengthen people, communities, and civic life?

## Supporting questions

### Accessibility

1. What happens when accessibility is introduced at the beginning of creative technology education rather than at the end?
2. How do disabled students experience belonging, authorship, and leadership in an accessibility-first studio?
3. Which documentation practices make student technology projects more usable, maintainable, and accessible?

### Physical computing

4. How does Arduino-based physical computing help students understand systems, embodiment, and environment?
5. How can sensor-based projects become accessible through sound, touch, plain language, and multimodal interfaces?

### Responsible AI

6. How can students design AI systems that preserve human judgment, provenance, and public accountability?
7. How do journalistic practices improve student understanding of AI-generated civic information?

### Community and civic systems

8. How can students work with partners without extracting data, stories, or labor?
9. What makes a student project publicly useful rather than merely technically impressive?

### Learning and pedagogy

10. What studio structures help students connect technical skill, ethical reasoning, and public purpose?
11. How can GitHub-based documentation become part of student learning rather than an afterthought?

### Speculative design

12. How can speculative design help students examine the social consequences of emerging technologies?
13. How can narrative prototyping support ethical reasoning about future systems?

## Hypothesis

Students who participate in an accessibility-first, public-interest creative technology studio will demonstrate stronger systems thinking, more inclusive design practices, better documentation habits, and more nuanced ethical reasoning than students who learn tools in isolation.
