# 01 — Executive Summary

Small Systems Lab is an accessibility-first creative technology studio that prepares students to build responsible, interdisciplinary systems for the public good.

The lab brings together Arduino and physical computing, responsible AI, civic media, journalism, environmental sensing, accessibility, open-source documentation, and speculative design.

## Research problem

Students are entering a world shaped by AI, climate instability, misinformation, disability access failures, and fragile public trust. Traditional creative technology education often separates tools from consequences: code from ethics, hardware from communities, accessibility from invention, and storytelling from systems.

Small Systems Lab responds by creating an integrated studio model where students learn to build, question, document, test, and evaluate systems in public-interest contexts.

## Research contribution

Small Systems Lab contributes a studio-based model for accessibility-first public-interest creative technology education.

The model combines:

- practice-based research
- participatory design
- accessible HCI
- physical computing
- responsible AI
- civic media
- environmental sensing
- speculative design
- open-source documentation

## Institutional value

Small Systems Lab can strengthen undergraduate and graduate creative technology education while creating a fundable research platform across ITP, IMA, IDM, Tandon, Journalism, Steinhardt, disability studies, and public-interest technology.

## Pilot

A first pilot can begin with:

- one undergraduate studio course
- one Arduino-based project pathway
- one public GitHub Pages site
- one student showcase
- one community or institutional partner
- a small student fellow cohort
- a research protocol for documenting learning outcomes and accessibility practices

## Long-term goal

The long-term goal is to grow Small Systems Lab into a recognized interdisciplinary teaching and research lab for accessible public-interest creative technology.
