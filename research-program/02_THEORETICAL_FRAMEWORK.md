# 02 — Theoretical Framework

## Overview

Small Systems Lab is grounded in the idea that technology is not merely a collection of tools. Technology is a social, material, cultural, and institutional system.

The lab's theoretical framework has six layers:

1. Accessibility as infrastructure
2. Small systems as research objects
3. Physical computing as embodied learning
4. Responsible AI as public accountability
5. Civic media as public knowledge practice
6. Speculative design as systems rehearsal

## 1. Accessibility as infrastructure

Accessibility is not treated as a checklist applied after a project is built. It is treated as infrastructure: a condition that shapes who can participate, what knowledge is valued, and how systems become usable.

This shifts the question from:

> How do we accommodate disabled users?

to:

> What would this system become if disabled people, neurodivergent people, multilingual users, and people with varied bodies and contexts were central from the start?

## 2. Small systems as research objects

A small system is a bounded, observable system that affects daily life.

Examples:

- a classroom attendance workflow
- a community garden watering system
- a public meeting transcript
- a museum label
- a wheelchair route
- a sensor kit
- a local news verification process

Small systems are useful research objects because they are concrete enough for students to prototype around, but complex enough to reveal power, access, communication, maintenance, and failure.

## 3. Physical computing as embodied learning

Physical computing gives students a way to understand systems through bodies, materials, sensors, and environments. Arduino becomes a pedagogical bridge between code and the physical world.

The student learns that computation is not only screen-based. It can be touched, heard, worn, repaired, powered, and embedded in public settings.

## 4. Responsible AI as public accountability

AI is treated as an editorial and civic system, not as magic automation.

Students examine:

- provenance
- source transparency
- human oversight
- misinformation
- accessibility
- translation
- risk
- public accountability

## 5. Civic media as public knowledge practice

Journalism is not only content production. It is a discipline of evidence, verification, narrative, accountability, and public service.

Small Systems Lab brings journalistic habits into creative technology: ask who is affected, what is known, what is missing, who benefits, and what evidence supports the claim.

## 6. Speculative design as systems rehearsal

Speculative design allows students to test possible futures before those futures arrive.

UMADA functions as a speculative research environment where students can examine governance, disability, communication, memory, cybernetics, infrastructure, and ethics through narrative prototypes.

## Theory of change

If students are trained to build accessible, accountable, interdisciplinary systems from the beginning, then they become more capable of creating technologies that serve public life rather than merely demonstrating technical novelty.
