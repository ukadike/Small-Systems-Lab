# 03 — Literature Review and Field Positioning

## Purpose

This literature review positions Small Systems Lab across several overlapping fields:

- human-computer interaction
- accessibility research
- disability studies
- participatory design
- physical computing education
- learning sciences
- civic technology
- responsible AI
- community-centered research
- speculative design

This is a living review. It should be expanded with citations, annotations, and full bibliographic entries.

## Accessibility and HCI

Accessibility research in HCI examines how computing systems include or exclude people with disabilities and how technology can be designed with diverse bodies, senses, cognitive styles, and social contexts in mind.

Small Systems Lab builds from this field but shifts emphasis from assistive technology alone to accessibility as a general design infrastructure for public-interest creative technology.

## Participatory and co-design methods

Participatory design and co-design emphasize the involvement of people affected by a technology in the design process. This is especially important in disability technology, where design without disabled people often reproduces exclusion.

Small Systems Lab treats students, disabled users, community partners, and domain experts as knowledge holders rather than passive recipients of design.

## Physical computing and embodied learning

Physical computing education uses sensors, actuators, microcontrollers, and tangible interaction to help learners connect computation to the physical world. Arduino is particularly valuable because it makes computation visible, testable, and materially grounded.

Small Systems Lab extends this approach by connecting physical computing to accessibility, community science, civic media, and public-interest systems.

## Civic technology and smart communities

Civic technology and smart communities research asks how computation, sensing, data, and public systems can support community life. Strong versions of this work require meaningful community engagement, not merely technological deployment.

Small Systems Lab emphasizes community-centered research, documentation, transparency, and public usefulness.

## Responsible AI and civic media

Responsible AI research examines accountability, transparency, human oversight, bias, and harm. Journalism contributes complementary methods of verification, sourcing, context, and public explanation.

Small Systems Lab combines these fields through Omoluabi, a research program for accessible civic intelligence.

## Speculative design

Speculative design uses future-oriented artifacts, narratives, and scenarios to examine values embedded in technological systems. It can help students rehearse ethical decisions before they become institutional realities.

UMADA is positioned as a speculative systems research program within the lab.
