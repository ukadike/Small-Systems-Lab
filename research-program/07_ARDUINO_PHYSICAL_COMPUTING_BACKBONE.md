# 07 — Arduino and Physical Computing Backbone

Arduino is the lab's first language of making.

It helps students understand that systems are not abstract. They are felt, touched, heard, carried, repaired, and shared.

## Why Arduino

Arduino makes computation physical and understandable. It lets students connect code to sensors, bodies, rooms, gardens, classrooms, museums, and public environments.

## Research role

Arduino supports the lab's research because it creates low-cost, inspectable, modifiable systems. Students can see how inputs become outputs, how data becomes action, and how design choices affect accessibility.

## Starter pathway

1. Arduino Uno R4 or compatible board
2. Breadboard and wires
3. Button, potentiometer, LEDs
4. Temperature and humidity sensor
5. Soil moisture sensor
6. Speaker or buzzer
7. Vibration motor
8. OLED display
9. ESP32 for wireless sensing
10. Raspberry Pi for local computing

## Research kit categories

- Accessible Input Kit
- Environmental Sensing Kit
- Multimodal Output Kit
- Civic Systems Kit
- Wearables and Haptics Kit
