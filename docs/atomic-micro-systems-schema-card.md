# Schema Card: Atomic Micro Systems

A quick-orientation card for this working theory. See

## Identity

- **Name:** Atomic Micro Systems
- **Origin:** Small Systems Lab
- **Author:** Adekemi "Kemi" Sijuwade-Ukadike
- **Status:** Working theory
- **Version:** 0.1
- **Date:** July 2026

## Purpose

Provide a conceptual and operational model for institutional transformation through dense, bounded sites of atomic potential rather than institution-wide change imposed as one enormous program.

## Core claim

Small does not mean weak. An apparently tiny institutional site can contain dense relationships and disproportionate capacity for attraction, transformation, replication, and consequence. Every node may become a field.

## Inputs

- institutional ambition;
- observed pressure points;
- workflows;
- participants and affected communities;
- rules and governance constraints;
- technologies and infrastructures;
- histories, habits, and informal practices;
- accessibility conditions;
- evidence from bounded experiments;
- public-trust and accountability requirements.

## Primary entities

- larger system;
- pressure point;
- nucleus;
- sandbox;
- atomic node;
- atomic potential;
- micro system;
- micro orb;
- filament/pathway;
- drop;
- seed;
- feedback;
- returnability.

## Transformations

1. Observe the larger field.
2. Locate a consequential pressure point.
3. Form a temporary multidisciplinary nucleus.
4. Place a bounded sandbox inside the nucleus.
5. Test one consequential assumption.
6. Gather evidence.
7. Permit evidence to alter the original ambition.
8. Form, reframe, connect, detach, dissolve, or stop micro systems.
9. Feed evidence back into the larger field.
10. Track drops and viable seeds that travel elsewhere.

## Outputs

- a clearer institutional question;
- a map of atomic nodes and relationships;
- evidence from a bounded sandbox;
- product requirements;
- governance requirements;
- accessibility requirements;
- workflow and operating-model changes;
- decisions to build, reframe, hold, defer, combine, transfer, or stop;
- connected micro systems;
- detached drops;
- viable seeds;
- new research questions.

## Human authority

Humans retain authority over:

- which pressure point is selected;
- who joins the nucleus;
- what may be tested;
- what evidence counts;
- when to pause or stop;
- how affected people participate;
- whether a micro system remains connected, scales, detaches, dissolves, or transfers;
- and how accountability persists after the nucleus dissolves.

## Accessibility baseline

- affected participants must be able to enter and influence the nucleus;
- sandbox conditions must include accessibility requirements from the beginning;
- any public representation must have text equivalents;
- no system should rely on color, motion, sound, or visual spatiality alone;
- AI-enabled micro systems must preserve returnability to human interpretation, correction, appeal, and responsibility.

## Evidence requirements

Evidence should be sufficient to determine whether the intervention:

- improves the work;
- shifts burden elsewhere;
- creates a new risk;
- excludes participants;
- damages trust;
- produces a viable micro system;
- reveals another pressure point;
- or should stop.

## Failure states

- treating the nucleus as permanent headquarters;
- reducing pathways to reporting lines;
- assuming every experiment should scale;
- using a sandbox as theater rather than an instrument;
- excluding people who live with the resulting operating model;
- confusing novelty with consequence;
- ignoring detached drops and seeds;
- presenting the atomic analogy as literal physics;
- erasing uncertainty through polished visual design.

## Stop conditions

Stop, hold, or reframe when:

- the question is not consequential enough;
- the sandbox cannot reproduce enough reality to generate useful evidence;
- affected participants cannot safely participate;
- governance or accessibility preconditions are absent;
- the intervention moves harm or labor elsewhere invisibly;
- no accountable human owner exists;
- evidence contradicts the original ambition;
- or continued development would convert a weak idea into an expensive obligation.

## Provenance

The concept note, working vocabulary, visual studies, revisions, and worked cases should remain versioned in Git. Any future implementation or institutional use should distinguish Kemi's original framework from later adaptations.

## Returnability

The model must preserve paths to:

- human interpretation;
- correction;
- appeal;
- accountability;
- reframing;
- re-entry;
- and stopping.
